Lemma C.9. Demushkin invariants of \(D_R\).

\(D_R\) is a Demushkin pro-\(2\) group of rank \(3\) with \(q(D_R)=2\text{.}\) In the basis of \(H^1(D_R,\F_2)\) dual to \((s,x,y)\text{,}\) the Gram matrix of the cup–Bockstein form is
\begin{equation} \begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\tag{C.7} \end{equation}
which is nonsingular.

Proof.
The presentation has three generators and one relator, so \(\dim H^1=3\) and \(\dim H^2\le1\text{.}\) From \(x^s=x[x,s]\) and \(y^s=y[y,s]\) one gets \([y,y^s]=[y,[y,s]]\) and hence
\begin{equation*} (x^s)^{-1}x^{-3}y^2[y,y^s]=[x,s]^{-1}\,x^{-4}\,y^2\,[y,[y,s]]. \end{equation*}
Both \(x^{-4}\) and \([y,[y,s]]\) lie in the third step of the filtration (C.9), so the initial form of the relator is \(y^2[x,s]\text{.}\) This reads off (C.7): the squared generator contributes the diagonal Bockstein entry at \(y\text{,}\) and the commutator contributes the off-diagonal pair at \((s,x)\text{.}\) The matrix is nonsingular, so \(\dim H^2=1\) and the cup product \(H^1\times H^1\to H^2\) is a perfect pairing, which is the Demushkin condition. Finally the exponent sums of the relator on \((s,x,y)\) are \((0,-4,2)\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} D_R^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_2^2\times\mathbb{Z}/2, \end{equation*}
and \(q(D_R)\text{,}\) the order of the torsion subgroup of the abelianization, is \(2\text{.}\)
🔗
At \(p=2\) the cup square \(u\smile u\) equals the Bockstein \(\beta(u)\) and is additive in \(u\text{,}\) so (C.7) is the Gram matrix of a symmetric bilinear form carrying the Bockstein on its diagonal, not the polar form of a quadratic form; invariants of quadratic type must not be read off from it.
