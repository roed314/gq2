Lemma 6.4. Uniqueness and isometry invariance of the normalized base class.

Let \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\text{,}\) \(H=H_V\text{,}\) and \(q\) be as in Lemma 6.3. Then
\begin{equation} H^1(H,\notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee)=0.\tag{6.20} \end{equation}
Any two classes in \(H^2(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\rtimes H,\F_2)\) which restrict to the class with square map \(q\) on \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\) and restrict trivially along the zero section \(H\hookrightarrow \notnfar{Vhead}{V}\rtimes H\) are equal. Consequently, changing the normalized factor set, the correction functions \(m_c\text{,}\) the half-orbit transversals, or the \(H\)-split regular embedding and retraction changes \(\notnfar{kappaq0}{\kappa_q^0}\) only by a normalized coboundary. In particular the base quadratic obstruction \(Q^0_{\Gamma,\notnfar{rhoEpi}{\rho}}\) is well-defined.
🔗
Moreover, every \(H\)-equivariant isometry \(u\) of \((\notnfar{Vhead}{V},q)\) fixes the normalized base class:
\begin{equation} (u\rtimes1)^*[\notnfar{kappaq0}{\kappa_q^0}]=[\notnfar{kappaq0}{\kappa_q^0}].\tag{6.21} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
We first prove the required cohomology vanishing, then use the low-degree Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre sequence for uniqueness, and finally deduce choice-independence and isometry invariance.
🔗
If \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\) is unramified, its faithful image \(H\) has odd order: the \(2\)-primary subgroup of the cyclic unramified image acts trivially on every simple characteristic-\(2\) module. Hence positive-degree \(\F_2[H]\)-cohomology vanishes by averaging.
🔗
Assume that \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\) is ramified, and let \(I\triangleleft H\) be the odd tame-inertia subgroup. The fixed space \((\notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee)^I\) is an \(H\)-submodule. It is zero, because \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\) is simple and inertia acts nontrivially. Since \(|I|\) is odd, \(H^j(I,\notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee)=0\) for \(j>0\text{.}\) The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence for \(1\to I\to H\to H/I\to1\) therefore gives [Bro, Ch. VII, §6]
\begin{equation*} H^n(H,\notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee)=0\qquad(n\ge0). \end{equation*}
In particular (6.20) holds in both cases.
🔗
Put \(G=\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\rtimes H\text{.}\) For an elementary abelian \(2\)-group \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\text{,}\) the square-map correspondence
\begin{equation*} H^2(\notnfar{Vhead}{V},\F_2)\xrightarrow{\sim} \{\text{quadratic maps }\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\to\F_2\} \end{equation*}
is an isomorphism: the polarization recovers the commutator form, and the square and commutator data determine the central extension class. Thus two classes with square map \(q\) have the same fibre restriction.
🔗
The low-degree Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre sequence for \(1\to \notnfar{Vhead}{V}\to G\to H\to1\) contains the exact segment [Bro, Cor. VII.6.4]
\begin{equation} H^2(H,\F_2)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{inf}} \ker\!\left(H^2(G,\F_2)\longrightarrow H^2(\notnfar{Vhead}{V},\F_2)\right) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{edge}}H^1(H,\notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee).\tag{6.22} \end{equation}
Let \(\xi\) be the difference of two classes with the stated normalizations. Its fibre restriction is zero. By (6.20) and exactness of (6.22), \(\xi=\operatorname{inf}(\delta)\) for some \(\delta\in H^2(H,\F_2)\text{.}\) Pullback along the zero section is a left inverse to inflation, while both classes vanish there. Hence \(\delta=0\) and \(\xi=0\text{.}\)
🔗
At the filtration-one level one can see the same cancellation directly. If \(\gamma=dh\) for \(h\in \notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee\text{,}\) then the normalized \(1\)-cochain
\begin{equation} \ell_h(v,c)=h(v)\tag{6.23} \end{equation}
on \(\notnfar{Vhead}{V}\rtimes H\) satisfies \(d\ell_h=\Gamma_{dh}\text{,}\) with \(\notnfar{GammaSrc}{\Gamma}\) as in (6.6).
🔗
If normalized cocycle representatives are used, their difference is \(da\) for a normalized \(1\)-cochain \(a\text{.}\) Since the difference vanishes on \(H\times H\text{,}\) \(a|_H\) is a \(1\)-cocycle. Subtracting its inflation makes \(a\) vanish on the zero section without changing \(da\text{.}\) Thus all choices in Lemma 6.3 give isomorphic normalized central extensions, and transport of lifts through that isomorphism leaves both the candidate relator obstruction and the local pullback class unchanged. This proves the choice-independence of \(Q^0_{\Gamma,\notnfar{rhoEpi}{\rho}}\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally, \((u\rtimes1)^*\notnfar{kappaq0}{\kappa_q^0}\) has the same fibre square map and the same zero-section restriction as \(\notnfar{kappaq0}{\kappa_q^0}\text{,}\) so uniqueness proves (6.21).
🔗
The uniqueness assertion is deliberately made over the faithful image \(H_V\text{.}\) After inflation to a larger quotient \(\notn{Cfin}{C}\) acting through \(H_V\text{,}\) the group \(H^1(\notn{Cfin}{C},\notnfar{Vhead}{V}^\vee)\) need not vanish; its contribution is precisely the filtration-one term \(\Gamma_\gamma\) in (6.5), and it remains part of the target-side affine data.
