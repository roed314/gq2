Lemma 9.2. Targets with only trivial module factors.

Suppose every chief factor of \(\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}\) is a trivial \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\)-module. Let \(H_2\) be the maximal \(2\)-quotient of \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\) and let \(N=\ker(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\to H_2)\text{.}\) Then \(N\) has odd order, has a unique normal lift \(\widetilde N\triangleleft \notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\) centralizing \(\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation} \notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\cong \notnfar{Htarget}{H}\times_{H_2}Q, \qquad Q=\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}/\widetilde N\text{ a finite $2$-group}.\tag{9.1} \end{equation}
Projection to \(Q\) identifies exact-image subgroups of \(\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\) projecting onto \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\) with subgroups \(Q'\le Q\) that map onto \(H_2\text{.}\)

Proof.
We construct and characterize the odd-order complement, identify \(\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\) as a fibre product, and then classify the exact-image subgroups.
🔗
The kernel \(N\) has odd order by Lemma 3.1. Let \(\notn{Pnorm}{P}\) be its inverse image in \(\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\text{.}\) Schur–Zassenhaus gives a complement \(\widetilde N\le \notn{Pnorm}{P}\text{.}\) Along a \(\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\)-chief series
\begin{equation*} 1=L_0\triangleleft L_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft L_m=\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}, \end{equation*}
triviality of every factor gives \([L_i,\widetilde N]\le L_{i-1}\text{.}\) Hence \([\notnfar{LY}{L_Y},{}_m\widetilde N]=1\text{.}\) Coprime action gives
\begin{equation*} [\notnfar{LY}{L_Y},\widetilde N]=[\notnfar{LY}{L_Y},\widetilde N,\widetilde N] =\cdots=[\notnfar{LY}{L_Y},{}_m\widetilde N]=1. \end{equation*}
Thus \(\notn{Pnorm}{P}=\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}\times\widetilde N\text{.}\) Schur–Zassenhaus conjugacy says that all complements are \(\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}\)-conjugate; since \(\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}\) centralizes \(\widetilde N\text{,}\) the complement is unique and therefore normal in \(\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\text{.}\)
🔗
In more detail: every element of the inverse image of \(N\) has a commuting factorization \(\tilde n\ell\text{,}\) with \(\tilde n\) of odd order and \(\ell\) of \(2\)-power order. Coprimality shows that such a product has odd order exactly when \(\ell=1\text{.}\) Thus \(\widetilde N\) is intrinsically the set of odd-order elements in this subgroup. This characterization is preserved by conjugation, giving normality and uniqueness at once.
🔗
The quotient \(Q=\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}/\widetilde N\) is a \(2\)-group. The two quotient maps define
\begin{equation*} \notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\longrightarrow \notnfar{Htarget}{H}\times_{H_2}Q. \end{equation*}
Its kernel lies in both \(\notnfar{LY}{L_Y}\) and \(\widetilde N\text{,}\) hence is trivial, and the two groups have the same order. This proves (9.1).
🔗
The same parity separation explains the decoration: every homomorphism to the exponent-two group \(E\) kills \(\widetilde N\text{,}\) so the decoration descends to \(Q\text{.}\) A subgroup of the resulting fibre product that surjects to both factors has kernels of coprime orders; the coprime subdirect-product lemma therefore forces it to be the whole fibre product.
🔗
If \(J\le \notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\) projects onto \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\) and \(Q'=\operatorname{pr}_Q(J)\text{,}\) then \(Q'\twoheadrightarrow H_2\text{.}\) Inside \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\times_{H_2}Q'\text{,}\) the subgroup \(J\) projects onto both factors. The two kernels have odd and \(2\)-power orders, so Lemma 9.1 gives \(J=\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\times_{H_2}Q'\text{.}\) Conversely, for every subgroup \(Q'\le Q\) mapping onto \(H_2\text{,}\) the fibre product \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\times_{H_2}Q'\) is a subgroup of \(\notnfar{Ytarget}{Y}\) projecting onto \(\notnfar{Htarget}{H}\text{.}\)
