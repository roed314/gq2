Proposition C.12. Marked identification of the pro-\(2\) quotient.

Let \(\nu_R:D_R\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_2\) be defined by \(\nu_R(s)=1\) and \(\nu_R(x)=\nu_R(y)=0\text{.}\) Then the marked pairs \((G_{\Qtwo}(2),\notn{nuur}{\nu_{\mathrm{ur}}})\) and \((D_R,\nu_R)\) are isomorphic.

Proof.
By Lemma C.9 and Proposition C.10 the group \(D_R\) satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition C.11, so \(D_R\cong \notn{D0}{D_0}\text{;}\) and \(\notn{D0}{D_0}\cong G_{\Qtwo}(2)\) is Proposition 1.1. It remains to match the markings. By Proposition C.6(ii) every continuous isomorphism intertwines the orientations, so the composite \(G_{\Qtwo}(2)\to D_R\) is determined on the abelianization up to those automorphisms of \(\notn{D0}{D_0}\) that preserve its orientation. Expressing the unramified character of the target in the coordinates of (C.6) leaves a pair of unknowns, and these are solved by a generation argument modulo \(2\) together with a parity computation for the \(s\)-coordinate modulo \(16\text{,}\) using the values (C.8). Composing with the resulting automorphism gives an isomorphism carrying \(\notn{nuur}{\nu_{\mathrm{ur}}}\) to \(\nu_R\text{.}\)
