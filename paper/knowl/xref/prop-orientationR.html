Proposition C.10. The orientation of \(D_R\).

\(D_R\) carries a unique continuous character \(\chi_R:D_R\to\mathbb{Z}_2^\times\) describing its action on the dualizing module. Its values on the generators are
\begin{equation} \chi_R(s)=S,\qquad \chi_R(x)=X,\qquad \chi_R(y)=Y=-X^2,\tag{C.8} \end{equation}
where \(X\in\mathbb{Z}_2\) is the unique root of \(Z^3+2Z^2+1\) with \(X\equiv1\pmod2\text{,}\) and \(S=-X^3/(X^2+X+1)\text{;}\) numerically \(X\equiv5\) and \(S\equiv13\pmod{16}\text{.}\) Moreover \(\chi_R\) is surjective, with image \(\{\pm1\}\times(1+4\mathbb{Z}_2)=\mathbb{Z}_2^\times\text{.}\)

Proof.
The orientation of a Demushkin group is characterized by the property that every \(\chi\)-crossed derivation of the free pro-\(2\) group kills the relator; see [Ser63, no. 9.3, pp. 153--154]. Evaluating the crossed derivations valued in \(\mathbb{Z}_2(\chi)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2^\times\) on the relator of (C.6) gives three coordinate equations, together with the relation \(Y^2=X^4\) forced by the exponent sums. The branch \(Y=+X^2\) is excluded, because it forces \((X-1)(X^2-X-1)=0\) with \(X^2-X-1\) a unit, while \(X=1\) is inconsistent with the remaining equation. On the branch \(Y=-X^2\) the system reduces to \(X^3+2X^2+1=0\text{,}\) which has a unique root congruent to \(1\) modulo \(2\) by Hensel’s lemma, and then \(S=-X^3/(X^2+X+1)\text{.}\) Surjectivity follows from Burnside’s basis theorem applied to the image, together with the fact that \(X\) is not a square modulo \(8\text{.}\)
