Remark 3.6. Why the orientation cannot be omitted.

An un-oriented marked-relator normalization with only the abelianized relation and quadratic initial form is false. Let \(F=\langle \notn{Agen}{A},\notn{Sgen}{S},\notn{Ygen}{Y}\rangle_{\mathrm{pro}-2}\) and define
\begin{equation*} \psi(\notn{Agen}{A})=AY^{-4}, \qquad \psi(\notn{Sgen}{S})=SY^2, \qquad \psi(\notn{Ygen}{Y})=\notn{Ygen}{Y}. \end{equation*}
This is an automorphism, with inverse \(\notn{Agen}{A}\mapsto AY^4\text{,}\) \(\notn{Sgen}{S}\mapsto SY^{-2}\text{,}\) \(\notn{Ygen}{Y}\mapsto \notn{Ygen}{Y}\text{.}\) For \(\notn{r0}{r_0}=\notn{Agen}{A}^2\notn{Sgen}{S}^4[\notn{Sgen}{S},\notn{Ygen}{Y}]\text{,}\) put \(r=\psi^{-1}(\notn{r0}{r_0})\text{.}\) On the free abelianization,
\begin{equation*} \psi_{\mathrm{ab}}(2\notn{barA}{\bar A}+4\notn{barS}{\bar S}) =2(\notn{barA}{\bar A}-4\notn{barY}{\bar Y})+4(\notn{barS}{\bar S}+2\notn{barY}{\bar Y}) =2\notn{barA}{\bar A}+4\notn{barS}{\bar S}. \end{equation*}
Moreover
\begin{equation*} r=(AY^4)^2(SY^{-2})^4[SY^{-2},\notn{Ygen}{Y}], \end{equation*}
so, modulo the third Zassenhaus term, its initial form is \(\notn{Agen}{A}^2+[\notn{Sgen}{S},\notn{Ygen}{Y}]\text{.}\) Thus \(r\) has the same abelianized relation and quadratic initial form as \(\notn{r0}{r_0}\text{,}\) and its quotient is abstractly \(\notn{D0}{D_0}\text{.}\) However, under the isomorphism induced by \(\psi\text{,}\) the transported canonical orientation takes \(\notn{Sgen}{S}\) to \(\notn{eta}{\eta}^2\ne1\text{.}\) Therefore no isomorphism from this quotient to \(\notn{D0}{D_0}\) which is the identity on \(\notn{Bab}{B_{\mathrm{ab}}}\) can exist. The orientation-preserving lifting statement Proposition 3.9, rather than the false un-oriented normalization, is the exact structural input needed below.
