Theorem C.15. Span theorem.

Let \(F\) be the free pro-\(2\) group on three generators, filtered by (C.9), and let \(\bar d_k\) be either (C.10) or (C.11) evaluated at the generators. For every \(k\ge3\text{,}\)
\begin{equation} Z_k(F)=\im\bar d_k +\angles{g_i^{2^{k-1}},\,g_j^{2^{k-1}}},\tag{C.12} \end{equation}
where \(g_i,g_j\) are the two untwisted generators: \(\notnfar{Sgen}{S},\notn{Ygen}{Y}\) for \(\notn{r0}{r_0}\) and \(s,x\) for \(r_2\text{.}\) The corresponding power of the twisted generator already lies in \(\im\bar d_k\text{.}\) The same identity holds in \(Z_k(D)\) for any quotient \(D\) of \(F\) and any generating triple, because the filtration (C.9) is verbal.

Proof.
Because the filtration is verbal, \(Z_k\) is generated by the classes of \(v^2\) and \([v,g]\) with \(v\in\lambda_{k-1}\) and \(g\in F\text{.}\) We reduce both families into the right-hand side of (C.12).
🔗
Bracket atoms. For fixed \(v\) the class of \([v,g]\) is multiplicative in \(g\) and central, so \(g\) may be taken to be a generator. Against either untwisted generator, \([v,g_i]\) is by definition a value of \(\bar d_k\text{,}\) namely the one with \(v\) in the corresponding slot and \(1\) in the others. Against the twisted generator \(t\) one has, also by definition, \([v,t]=(v^2)^{-1}\bar d_k(v,1,1)\text{.}\) So the bracket atoms lie in \(\im\bar d_k\) together with the square atoms.
🔗
Square atoms, by induction on \(k\text{.}\) Let \(v\in\lambda_{k-1}\text{.}\) By the statement one level down, the image of \(v\) in \(Z_{k-1}\) is a product of values of \(\bar d_{k-1}\) and of the two level-\((k-1)\) powers. Lift that factorization back into \(\lambda_{k-1}\text{;}\) the ambiguity is an element of \(Z_k\text{,}\) a central involution, which dies on squaring. Squaring is multiplicative on \(\lambda_{k-1}\)-classes because \([\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k-1}]\subseteq\lambda_{k+1}\text{,}\) so it suffices to square each factor. A lifted power \(g_i^{2^{k-2}}\) squares to the level-\(k\) power \(g_i^{2^{k-1}}\text{,}\) and a lifted value of \(\bar d_{k-1}\) squares to a value of \(\bar d_k\) by the transport identity
\begin{equation} \bar d_k(w)^2=\bar d_k(w^2),\tag{C.13} \end{equation}
read slotwise. Its proof is the same layer calculus: the four factors of \(\bar d_k(w)\) lie one step deeper, squares of products of such elements are products of squares, and the correction term in \([u,g]^2=[u^2,g]\) is \([[u,g],u]\in[\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k-2}]\subseteq\lambda_{2k-3}\text{,}\) which is trivial in \(Q_{k+1}\) as soon as \(k\ge4\text{.}\) This closes the induction, and no termination measure beyond \(k\) is needed, because the recursion is exactly the statement one level down.
🔗
Base case. For \(k=3\) the computation takes place in \(Q_4\text{,}\) where the layer calculus is exact: \((uv)^4=u^4v^4[v,u]^2\text{,}\) and \([u,v]^2\) is bimultiplicative. Both atom families then close under the same reductions, using \([u^2,z]=[u,z][[u,z],u][u,z]\) for the squares of bracket atoms, and the diagonal identity \(t^4=\bar d_3(t^2,1,1)\text{,}\) valid because \(t\) commutes with \(t^2\text{,}\) for the twisted generator’s fourth power.
